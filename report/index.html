Nifty Trend Spread

Sell the ATM option in the direction of the trend and buy a cheaper option further out as the hedge. A bullish signal opens a bull put spread; a bearish signal opens a bear call spread. Signals come from EMA, MACD and Supertrend on the Nifty chart. This page covers the rules, the risk plan at 1:2, real backtest results on 5, 10 and 15 minute candles, how to set it up in an algo builder, and the margin for one lot.

    Overview

    How the strategy works

    Every candle close runs the same checks. A trade opens only when all three indicators agree, and it always goes in with the hedge leg first. Each trade has a stop-loss and a target fixed at 1:2, a trailing rule, and a hard exit at 15:15.

    Structure

    The two spreads

    Strikes are in steps of 50. ATM is the strike nearest to Nifty spot at entry. The hedge sits a fixed distance away from the sold strike, so the most you can lose is capped at (width − credit) × 65 per lot.

        Capital

        Margin for one lot

        Nifty lot size is 65 from January 2026. Selling one ATM option alone (naked) blocks roughly 8–9% of the contract value. Buying the hedge first cuts this sharply, because the most the position can lose is capped.

        Practical capital per lot: keep about ₹75,000–₹1,00,000 in the account per lot. That covers margin, the hedge premium, a string of stop-losses and margin spikes on volatile days. Do not trade the sold leg on its own expiry day: SEBI adds an extra 2% exposure margin on short options expiring that day, and gamma risk is highest then. The backtest rolls to the next week’s expiry on expiry day.
