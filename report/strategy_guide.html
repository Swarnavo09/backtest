Nifty Intraday Spread Strategy

A plain-English guide · trade Monday, Tuesday and Thursday · every trade closed by 3:00 PM

1. The idea in one minute

On each of your trading days you wait for Nifty to show a clear direction. When it does, you place a two-option trade. You sell one option and buy a cheaper one further away as protection. You collect money (a "premium") when you open the trade. If Nifty goes your way or stays quiet, the options lose value during the day and you keep part of that money. If Nifty moves against you, the option you bought stops the loss from becoming huge, and a stop-loss closes the trade early anyway. Everything is closed by 3:00 PM. Nothing is held overnight.

  • If Nifty looks like it is going up: sell a put, buy a cheaper put (called a bull put spread).
  • If Nifty looks like it is going down: sell a call, buy a cheaper call (called a bear call spread).

2. Words used in this guide

LotOne lot of Nifty options is 65 quantity. 4 lots = 260, 6 lots = 390.
ATM"At the money": the strike nearest to where Nifty is trading now (strikes are 50 apart).
OTM 300A strike 300 points away from ATM, further from the current price. Cheaper than ATM.
PremiumThe price of an option. Selling an option gives you the premium. Buying costs you the premium.
HedgeThe cheaper option you buy to cap your loss.
EMAA smoothed average of recent prices. When a fast EMA (9) is above a slow EMA (21), the trend is up.
SupertrendA line on the chart that sits below the price in an uptrend and above it in a downtrend.
Stop-lossThe loss amount at which you close the trade, no matter what.
MISThe intraday order type. Positions are closed the same day.

3. The rules, step by step

1
Pick the days. Trade on Monday, Tuesday and Thursday only. Do nothing on Wednesday and Friday. One trade a day at most.
2
Check the bigger direction (30-minute chart).
EMA 9 above EMA 21 = bullish day. EMA 9 below EMA 21 = bearish day.
3
Wait for the entry signal (15-minute chart). A signal needs all of these to be true at the close of a 15-minute candle:
BullishBearish
Price is above the Supertrend line (settings 10 and 3)Price is below the Supertrend line
EMA 9 is above EMA 21EMA 9 is below EMA 21
Price is above EMA 21Price is below EMA 21
The 30-minute check from step 2 is bullishThe 30-minute check from step 2 is bearish
The signal candle must close between 9:30 AM and 1:30 PM. If everything already agrees at 9:30, you enter then. After 1:30 PM, no new trade.
4
Place the two orders (buy the protection first).
Bullish dayBearish day
First: BUY the hedgePut at ATM − 300Call at ATM + 300
Then: SELLPut at ATMCall at ATM
Use the nearest weekly expiry, including on expiry day itself. Same quantity on both: 260 for 4 lots, 390 for 6 lots. Use limit orders to keep your fill price close to the quote.
5
Close the trade at whichever comes first:
  • Loss limit: your total loss reaches ₹3,000 per lot (₹12,000 for 4 lots, ₹18,000 for 6 lots). Close both options.
  • Trend turns: the 30-minute EMA 9 crosses to the other side of EMA 21 (the day's direction flips). Close both options.
  • Time: 3:00 PM. Close both options.
When closing, buy back the option you sold first, then sell the protection.
What you do not use: no profit target and no trailing stop. Both were tested and added nothing, because most winning trades are still gaining at 3:00 PM.

A worked example (made-up numbers to show how it works)

It is a Tuesday at 10:15 AM, Nifty is at 25,230, the 30-minute trend is up and every 15-minute condition is met, so ATM is 25,250. You buy the 24,950 put at ₹20 and sell the 25,250 put at ₹105. You collect 85 points, which is about ₹33,150 at 6 lots. If Nifty stays above 25,250 and the options decay, you might buy the spread back at about 40 by 3:00 PM and keep roughly 45 points, about ₹17,500. If Nifty falls and your loss reaches ₹18,000, you close the trade there. The most you could ever lose on this trade, even if the stop failed, is the 300-point gap minus the 85 points collected: 215 points, about ₹84,000. That is the hedge doing its job.

6. Important: which days really make the money

In the past, expiry day (the last day of a weekly option contract) has been where most of the profit comes from, because options lose value fastest that day. NSE moved the Nifty weekly expiry from Thursday to Tuesday in September 2025. The table shows what that did to each day (6 lots):

(Thursday was expiry)		Sep 2025 onward

All three together+₹6,44,874+₹5,52,266
What this means for you. Thursday made about ₹7.3 lakh of the 6-lot profit, but nearly all of it came while Thursday was expiry day. Since September 2025, Thursday has roughly broken even and Tuesday (now expiry day) and Monday (the day before) are earning the profit. Mon + Tue + Thu still made money in both periods, so it is a reasonable plan. Going forward, expect Tuesday to do most of the work, and treat Thursday as a quiet day that neither helps nor hurts much. A version trading only Monday and Tuesday made +₹4.66 lakh at 6 lots over the full period, but lost money until August 2025, so it has less history behind it.

9. Limits and honest warnings

  • Option prices in the test are partly modelled. Each option starts from its real closing price on NSE, but prices during the day were calculated from that, not recorded tick by tick. Real results can differ.
  • The test assumes decent fills. It allows ½ point of slippage on every order. Use limit orders. If your average slippage is above about 1 point, the profit mostly disappears.
  • It rests on a small number of strong periods. 2026 alone made about ₹3.2 lakh (4 lots) of the ₹7.8 lakh five-year total. A quieter market can mean a few flat years.
  • Expiry-day dependence. If NSE changes the expiry day again, re-check which days work.
  • The stop can slip. In a fast market or a gap, your exit can be worse than the planned loss. The hedge limits the damage but it is not zero.
  • Lot size and margin rules change. The test uses today's lot size of 65 for all years.
  • SEBI's own study found that about 9 in 10 individual F&O traders lost money over FY22–FY24. This guide is a record of a backtest. It is not investment advice.

Checklist before you start

  • Run AlgoRooms' own backtest and check you see about 2–3 trades a week.
  • Paper-trade (or trade 1 lot) for 4–8 weeks and check your real fills against the quote.
  • Check the Leg 2 strike says OTM 300, not "ATM + 300", so it flips sides correctly on bearish days.
  • Confirm the margin on expiry day before you place the first trade at full size.
  • Keep the loss limit in place every single day, with no exceptions.

Backtest period: 3 January 2022 to 19 November 2026 on Nifty 15-minute data. Settings were chosen using 2022–2024 and confirmed on 2025–2026. Costs include brokerage, STT, exchange fees, GST, stamp duty and slippage.
