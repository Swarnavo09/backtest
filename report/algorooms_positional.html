What this builds: on a long (bullish) signal both legs are CE: buy ATM CE and sell the CE 300 pts higher (a bull call spread). On a short (bearish) signal both are PE: buy ATM PE and sell the PE 300 pts lower (a bear put spread). The most you can lose is the premium paid, about ₹15–20k for 2 lots. This is the opposite of the intraday strategy: here Leg 1 is BUY and Leg 2 is SELL.
Expiry: the backtest used the nearest weekly with at least 8 days left. NEXT WEEKLY is the closest AlgoRooms choice. On a Monday or Tuesday it can leave only 7–8 days; that’s fine.

Why 09:15 AM start: entries use a crossover (screen 2), which only fires on the hourly candle where the cross happens. Checking all day means AlgoRooms catches the cross whenever it happens. The backtest acted the next morning instead, so entries will sometimes come a few hours earlier than tested. If AlgoRooms’ CNC mode adds an automatic square-off time, set it so positions are not closed each day. They are meant to be held until the exit rules on screens 2–3 fire.

Why “Crosses Above / Below” here: the backtest only entered when the trend setup was new. Re-entering straight away while the old setup was still showing turned a +₹2.39 L strategy into a loser in 2025–26. Using a crossover means that after a stop-loss, AlgoRooms waits for a fresh cross before the next trade. Rows 1 keep you on the trend side.
If your chart history is short: EMA 350 on 1H needs about 50 trading days of history. AlgoRooms normally loads this itself; if the line looks wrong, check the data range.

₹10,000 loss limit: in the backtest, a 1% adverse Nifty close cost about ₹9,700–10,300 at 2 lots. Scale it with lots: about ₹5,000 per lot.
Exit before expiry (important): the backtest closed every trade at the close of the day before expiry. If AlgoRooms has an “exit before expiry” or “square off N days before expiry” option, set it to 1 day. If it doesn’t, close the trade yourself at about 03:15 PM on the day before expiry. Don’t let a positional spread go into expiry day.
